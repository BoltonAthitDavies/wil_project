\chapter[CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS]{Conclusion and Recommendations}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This project set out to replace infrastructure-based navigation with a
vision-based pipeline that stays accurate when the warehouse changes. At the
reporting cutoff the pipeline exists with either of two front ends behind one
RTAB-Map back end, the two experiments that judge it are defined for both
domains, and the evidence is drawn from one simulated warehouse in two layouts,
recorded with and without traffic, and from four real recordings with an
optical-tracker reference. Both simulation slots and the real-robot slot of
Experiment~1 hold data; the real-robot slot of Experiment~2, the prior map and
relocalization on it, holds none. The conclusions below are bounded
by the validity limits recorded beside each slot in \cref{chap:results} and
collected in \cref{tab:limitations}.

\section{Conclusions by Experiment}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection{Experiment 1: is vision earning its place?}

On the four realistic recordings a five-state wheel-plus-gyroscope EKF, run on
the inertial noise model the visual systems themselves declare, localises to
\SIrange{0.68}{2.80}{\metre} aligned ATE and is behind VINS-Fusion
(\SIrange{0.22}{0.66}{\metre}) on every recording
(\cref{tab:exp1-accuracy}). The same filter on a noise model measured from
each recording holds \SIrange{0.21}{0.58}{\metre} and would be ahead of
VINS-Fusion on three of four, so the whole of the gap is the noise model and
none of it the architecture: over this range the inertial noise model is worth
more than the choice of estimator, and the visual systems have never been
tuned to this platform. Two results survive that control. The proprioceptive
sensors fail in complementary ways, the IMU holding heading to under
\SI{2}{\degree} and losing \SIrange{12}{23}{\metre} of position, the wheels
the reverse, and the filter is better than either in both quantities because it
observes the gyroscope bias rather than the heading. And every dead-reckoning
covariance is overconfident, by one to three orders of magnitude. Against the
project's target of \SI{0.05}{\metre} ATE, no estimator qualifies --- on the I
layout what keeps ORB-SLAM3 above it is height drift, one run reaching
\SI{0.044}{\metre} in the floor plane, while on the U layout the planar error
itself is \SIrange{0.10}{0.33}{\metre} (a post-hoc split,
\cref{tab:exp1-planar-ate}); against the
\SI{0.1}{\metre} RPE target at \SI{1}{\second}, every visual run does on
jump-free pairs, as do wheel odometry and the measured-noise filter, while the
IMU and the filter on the visual systems' noise model do not. The
comparison is from one run per cell, on simulated encoders that barely slip,
and with traffic present as a condition rather than a factor.

On the real robot the slot now holds the comparison it exists for
(\cref{sec:exp1-real}). Wheel+gyro dead reckoning, with the feedback speed
scaled by one constant fitted against the tracker on \repo{realv7_000} and
held, localises the robot to \SI{0.26}{} and \SI{0.22}{\metre} on the other
two \repo{realv7} recordings and \SI{0.46}{\metre} on \repo{realv6}, where
the best ORB-SLAM3 run reads \SI{0.44}{}, \SI{0.17}{} and \SI{0.46}{\metre}.
On routes of \SIrange{22}{37}{\metre} the baseline and ORB-SLAM3 are in the
same accuracy class: the baseline leads on one held recording, ties on another, and
is beaten on the third by the runs that followed the initialisation patch of
\cref{sec:exp1-real}. VINS-Fusion, once its field-of-view and extrinsic
defects were corrected, reads \SI{0.46}{}, \SI{0.68}{} and
\SI{0.37}{\metre} on the same three: a valid comparison at last, and third of
the three families on every recording. Neither result is the one a
\SI{20}{\metre} route can settle. The baseline carries one tuned constant and no yaw-rate observation,
so no EKF could be formed, and both sides are scored with the marker lever arm
unmeasured.

The visual half of the same experiment is scored on the same four recordings
(\cref{sec:results-validity}). With physically simulated traffic
neither pipeline loses tracking. VINS-Fusion holds \SIrange{0.22}{0.66}{\metre}
with no jump and is worst on the U layout with traffic, though the single
excursion in that run's error trace is smaller than the largest on its static
twin, so the ranking is not traced to the traffic; ORB-SLAM3 holds \SIrange{0.14}{0.52}{\metre}
in eleven runs of sixteen, and its five divergences are the loop closer's, in
both directions: a false closure between the repeated aisles of the I layout
that both front ends accept at the same frame with a \SI{14}{\metre}
correction, putting them \SIrange{4.8}{6.0}{\metre} off where the closure-free
runs hold half a metre, and a vertical drift on the same layout with traffic
that only an accepted closure removes (\cref{tab:realistic-baseline}). The
front end changes throughput, \SIrange{97}{99}{\percent} of frames kept with
the GPU extractor against \SIrange{72}{94}{\percent} with the CPU one, and not
accuracy. ORB-SLAM3's \SIrange{13}{19}{\degree} rotational RMSE is a
frame convention, not its tracking: the node publishes in the camera optical
frame until inertial initialisation, and the \SIrange{89.8}{90.2}{\degree} step
into the gravity-aligned frame at \SIrange{2.47}{2.64}{\second} is the whole of
that column. Measured after it, the rotational RMSE is
\SIrange{0.27}{3.14}{\degree} on the three recordings free of a false closure,
at or below VINS-Fusion's \SIrange{1.18}{3.57}{\degree}. Both the step and
what survives it belong to the method's own initialisation \cite{campos2021}:
the map is rotated into the estimated gravity frame once, at ten keyframes,
and that direction is re-estimated only twice more, the second time with the
prior on the IMU biases removed, after which the stereo-inertial path offers
it no further refinement. The residual pitch and roll error divides on the
yaw available inside that window, \SIrange{0.242}{0.723}{\degree} on the two
recordings that turn within it against \SIrange{0.455}{3.709}{\degree} on the
two that do not, although with two routes on each side that division is not
yet separated from the layout. Eighteen repeated runs on the two mapping recordings
measure what each pipeline costs: VINS-Fusion optimises in
\SI{44}{\milli\second} against ORB-SLAM3's \SIrange{211}{406}{\milli\second}
of local mapping and holds its state in a quarter of the memory, for more
than twice the processor time, about eight cores of one against three and a
half (\cref{tab:mapping-resources}). Neither pipeline is cheaper outright,
and the choice between them is a memory-against-cores trade.

On the real robot ORB-SLAM3 tracks every recording: a provisional
\SIrange{0.404}{0.480}{\metre} planar ATE on \repo{realv6_000} and
\SIrange{0.192}{0.445}{\metre} across the three \repo{realv7} recordings, all
sixteen runs scored with no repair applied and with the marker lever arm
unmeasured (\cref{sec:exp1-real}). The four configurations of a recording
agree to \SI{0.02}{\metre}, so on this rig neither the front end nor the loop
closer moves the figure; the recording does, by a factor of two. The cause of
the IMU staying uninitialised on the \repo{realv7} recordings is identified
in the upstream code, patched in the fork and exercised on every run reported.
VINS-Fusion completes all three \repo{realv7} recordings at
\SIrange{0.374}{0.676}{\metre}, behind ORB-SLAM3 and the wheel+gyro
baseline, and only once the fisheye field of view is masked at
\SI{70}{\degree} and the stereo extrinsic frozen; the calibration's
non-monotonic projection past \SI{109}{\degree} is the defect under that
workaround, and the operative mechanism under it is the unbounded normalised
coordinate a feature acquires as its incidence angle approaches
\SI{90}{\degree}, not the unprojectable band, which is measured to be black.
Every estimator sits \SIrange{0.2}{0.4}{\metre} ahead of the
tracker marker along the path and the baseline does not, so measuring the
marker-to-IMU lever arm is the largest correction still open on the real-rig
figures.

How much harder the real robot is than the simulation depends entirely on how
the question is asked, and the two domains were measured to find out
(\cref{sec:exp1-real}). The real recordings are not visually poorer: the frames
carry \SI{169}{\percent} of the simulation's image gradient and
\SI{151}{\percent} of its corners per megapixel, neither estimator starved in
any of the three recordings, and the simulation's one identified VINS-Fusion
failure mode did not occur. What differs is the motion. The robot travels at
\SI{0.28}{\metre\per\second} against \SI{1.50}{}, stands still
\SI{18}{\percent} of the time, and therefore integrates \num{5.8} times more
inertial time for every metre of ground it covers. Charged per metre the real
rig drifts \num{10.5} times as fast as the simulation; charged per second of
operation, \num{1.9} times, and the ratio between those two readings is the
integration factor itself. The real rig is about twice as hard per second, not
twelve times, and the way to improve its numbers is the terms that accumulate
with time --- the lever arm, the inertial calibration, the standing periods ---
rather than richer image features, of which the real camera already supplies
more than the pipelines use.

\subsection{Experiment 2: does the robot localize on the map it built?}

\textbf{The map.} Where tracking is sound, the mapping pipeline is: the
tiny-warehouse map is \SI{96.6}{\percent} precise in what it marks occupied,
recovers \SI{89.5}{\percent} of the surfaces it observed, and contains no blind
cells and \SI{0.7}{\percent} proven false obstacles along an independently
driven route (\cref{tab:map-geometry,tab:map-drivability}), passing all three
map targets of \cref{tab:targets}; the drivability half of that result is
dated, because its independent route is lost. The two closure-free priors of
the realistic warehouse, the maps part~B relocalises on, fail the same targets,
fixed before scoring: precision \SIrange{77.8}{82.2}{\percent}, observed recall
\SIrange{74.9}{77.1}{\percent}, false obstacles \SIrange{5.0}{10.2}{\percent}
against the static-twin probes (\cref{sec:map-realistic}). Their poses are
accurate, within \SIrange{0.13}{0.33}{\metre} rms, so the failure is grid
construction: the false obstacles are phantoms laid during the inertial
initialisation transient at the spawn and wall bands displaced from their true
lines by uncorrected drift. On the large
warehouse the attempt failed through tracking loss in repeated structure and
its artefacts were not retained (\cref{sec:map-large}).

\textbf{Relocalization on it.} Six live runs in the two static realistic
worlds track ground truth to a median of \SIrange{0.13}{0.21}{\metre} per run
over hand-driven routes of \SIrange{21}{148}{\metre}, with a minimum clearance
of \SI{0.68}{\metre} and no collision (\cref{sec:exp4-sim}). Five of the six
meet the availability target and four the continuity target. One run crossed a
metre, for \SI{6.5}{\second}, and recovered. The error is a constant offset
rather than scatter, and its magnitude is that of the prior's own nodes along
the route (\SIrange{0.09}{0.14}{\metre}; the pose is \numrange{1.0}{1.7} times
that). Relocalization is therefore \emph{map-bounded in magnitude}; inside a
run, places with worse nodes or a worse grid carry no larger error, so whether
a better map would lower it remains untested. The same
initialisation transient that lays the map's blocking phantoms also latches the
back end's first fix \SIrange{73}{76}{\degree} wrong until it is held back for
\SI{5}{\second}: one transient, two symptoms, one prescription.

What remains planned is goal-reaching. No goal was commanded in any run, so
nothing yet speaks to planning, to recovery, or to the question the map scores
raise but cannot settle --- whether a \SIrange{5.0}{10.2}{\percent}
false-obstacle rate blocks a route or merely lengthens it. Neither traffic
world is measured, so relocalization in a dynamic warehouse is untested. Every
cell is a single run of one configuration, so nothing here measures run-to-run
spread and no comparison within \cref{tab:exp4-tracking} is evidence.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

The limitations of \cref{tab:limitations} carry over without softening. Eight
of them bound the conclusions above most tightly.

\begin{itemize}
\item Every simulated cell holds one run, so no between-cell difference is an
      effect; the one run-to-run figure in the report is the agreement of
      ORB-SLAM3's two front ends on where and how its loop closer fails.
\item No two recordings share a route, so traffic is a condition of a
      recording, not a tested factor.
\item Every result is from one laptop at one replay rate, and the
      mapping-recording repeats were not interleaved between configurations,
      so nothing here is evidence of real-time capability on the robot's own
      computer.
\item The non-visual baseline rests on idealised encoders in simulation and on
      a speed scale fitted against the truth on one recording on the real
      robot.
\item The real recordings were driven at \SI{0.28}{\metre\per\second}, a
      fifth of the working speed, so the trajectory targets are not exercised
      at the design condition on the robot.
\item The simulated worlds' perimeter walls and floor carry no texture, and
      the visual estimators' excursions sit where the camera faces them
      (\cref{fig:exp1-vins-starvation}); a textured world, or real footage,
      would place that error elsewhere or not at all.
\item The traffic recordings contain one four-second episode of the
      configuration that actually degrades the estimators, a vehicle driving
      ahead of the robot (\cref{fig:exp1-jack-ahead}); every other mover in
      view costs nothing. The dynamic-object hypothesis is exercised by four
      seconds of 117.
\item The real-robot numbers are planar and provisional until the
      marker-to-IMU lever arm is measured, and rest on one tracker export that
      fails the validity rules and three that pass. VINS-Fusion reaches a
      valid run on the three passing recordings only after the fisheye field of
      view is masked at \SI{70}{\degree}, which is a workaround for a
      calibration that cannot describe a tenth of every frame.
\end{itemize}

The prior map has no real-robot evidence at all, and the finding that traffic
does not drive the global error rests on simulated traffic alone.

\section{Recommendations and Next Steps}
\label{sec:recommendations}

\textbf{Command goals on the prior map.} Fix a goal set in each realistic
world, write the scorer that reads the Nav2 action results and the
ground-truth pose into the goal-reaching measures of
\cref{sec:nav-eval-method}, and run each world against each of its two map
configurations. The system exists and is launchable; goal-reaching is the half
of Experiment~2 that has never been scored.

\textbf{Bound the loop closer.} Add a maximum correction against the inertial
drift bound, or a covisibility-consistency window, and test it on
\repo{realistic_001}, where the \SI{14}{\metre} false closure is reproducible
in both front ends.

\textbf{Follow a vehicle.} Record a route on which a pallet jack leads the
robot down an aisle for tens of seconds rather than four, so that the one
configuration shown to degrade both estimators is tested at the length a
warehouse would impose. If the inertial prior stops absorbing it at that
length, dynamic-object handling becomes worth adding.

\textbf{Excite the gravity estimate, and separate it from the layout.} Record
the I layout with an opening manoeuvre that turns inside the first fifteen
seconds, so that the recordings differ in the yaw available to the inertial
initialisation while the route is held. On the present four recordings that
excitation is confounded with the layout, and this is the one measurement
that would decide whether the residual gravity error is the cause of the
vertical drift or a companion of it.

\textbf{Fill the real-robot slots in the order the simulation results
suggest.} Measure the marker-to-IMU lever arm, which every real accuracy
figure waits on; recalibrate the fisheye so that VINS-Fusion no longer needs
its field of view masked; replace the fitted wheel-speed scale by a measured
one and record the steering angle as a measurement so that the EKF can be
formed with the \SI{0.60}{\metre} wheelbase; drive a recording at the working
speed; and build a first real-imagery prior for Experiment~2.

\textbf{Tune the visual systems to the platform.} The cheapest remaining
improvement is a measured inertial noise model for the visual estimators:
Experiment~1 shows the noise model is worth more than the estimator.

\textbf{Start mapping after the inertial transient}, which the data predict
would remove the spawn phantoms behind the realistic maps' false obstacles,
and score any map against a recording that did not build it.

\textbf{Record every behaviour-changing switch in the run manifest}, refuse an
output directory whose name disagrees with its dataset, repeat every
nondeterministic configuration at least three times, and retain the artefacts
of failed attempts.

\textbf{Future direction.} Integrated relocalisation against a saved map,
global pose-graph optimisation with a bounded loop closer, map reconstruction
after objects have been moved, and alignment and merging of maps across
sessions remain future work; path planning stays outside the project.
