\chapter[CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS]{Conclusion and Recommendations}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This project set out to replace infrastructure-based navigation with a
vision-based pipeline that stays accurate when the warehouse changes, and to
decide between two ways of building it. At the reporting cutoff the pipeline
exists in both forms, the four experiments that judge it are defined for both
domains, and the evidence is drawn from one simulated warehouse in two layouts,
recorded with and without traffic, and from four real recordings with an
optical-tracker reference. All four simulation slots and two real-robot slots hold
data; the two hypotheses the project is named for, dynamic-object filtering and
dense mapping on the real robot, hold none. The conclusions below are bounded
by the validity limits recorded beside each slot in \cref{chap:results} and
collected in \cref{tab:limitations}.

\section{Conclusions by Experiment}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection{Experiment 1: is vision earning its place?}

On the four realistic recordings a five-state wheel-plus-gyroscope EKF, run on
the inertial noise model the visual systems themselves declare, localises to
\SIrange{0.68}{2.80}{\metre} aligned ATE and is behind VINS-Fusion
(\SIrange{0.22}{0.66}{\metre}) on every recording
(\cref{tab:exp1-accuracy}). The same filter on a noise model measured from
each recording holds \SIrange{0.21}{0.58}{\metre} and would be ahead of
VINS-Fusion on three of four, so the whole of the gap is the noise model and
none of it the architecture: over this range the inertial noise model is worth
more than the choice of estimator, and the visual systems have never been
tuned to this platform. Two results survive that control. The proprioceptive
sensors fail in complementary ways, the IMU holding heading to under
\SI{2}{\degree} and losing \SIrange{12}{23}{\metre} of position, the wheels
the reverse, and the filter is better than either in both quantities because it
observes the gyroscope bias rather than the heading. And every dead-reckoning
covariance is overconfident, by one to three orders of magnitude. Against the
project's target of \SI{0.05}{\metre} ATE, no estimator qualifies; against the
\SI{1.5}{\metre} RPE target at \SI{1}{\second}, all but the IMU do. The
comparison is from one run per cell, on simulated encoders that barely slip,
and with traffic present as a condition rather than a factor.

On the real robot the slot now holds the comparison it exists for
(\cref{sec:exp1-real}). Wheel+gyro dead reckoning, with the feedback speed
scaled by one constant fitted against the tracker on \repo{realv7_000} and
held, localises the robot to \SI{0.26}{} and \SI{0.22}{\metre} on the other
two \repo{realv7} recordings and \SI{0.46}{\metre} on \repo{realv6}, where
the best ORB-SLAM3 run reads \SI{0.44}{}, \SI{0.17}{} and \SI{0.46}{\metre}.
On routes of \SIrange{22}{37}{\metre} the two families are in the same
accuracy class: the baseline leads on one held recording, ties on another, and
is beaten on the third by the runs that followed the initialisation patch of
\cref{sec:exp1-real}. Neither result is the one a \SI{20}{\metre} route can
settle. The baseline carries one tuned constant and no yaw-rate observation,
so no EKF could be formed, and both sides are scored with the marker lever arm
unmeasured.

\subsection{Experiment 2: how good is the detector?}

The four-class detector is reliable at naming and unreliable at finding
(\cref{sec:yolo-performance}): over 12\,698 frames of the four realistic
recordings, pooled precision 0.747, class F1 0.994 given a match, recall
0.502 over the truth boxes the exemptions keep and 0.288 over everything in
view, and moving-object recall 0.288 on the pallet jacks. The miss is a size
effect, recall rising from 0.02 to 0.64 with the truth box's side, and
three quarters of the false positives are boxes a little off the right
object. That moving-object recall is the ceiling of any filtering benefit
downstream: a mask built on this detector covers under a third of the moving
pallet jacks' frames and none of the walking people, which are simulator
actors outside its classes and outside the score. The real recordings have no
annotated frames, so the real-robot slot stays planned.


The visual half of the same experiment is scored on the same four recordings
(\cref{sec:results-validity}). With physically simulated traffic
neither pipeline loses tracking. VINS-Fusion holds \SIrange{0.22}{0.66}{\metre}
with no jump and is worst on the U layout with traffic, where its error rises
on the aisles the pallet jacks work; ORB-SLAM3 holds \SIrange{0.14}{0.52}{\metre}
in eleven runs of sixteen, and its five divergences are the loop closer's, in
both directions: a false closure between the repeated aisles of the I layout
that both front ends accept at the same frame with a \SI{14}{\metre}
correction, putting them \SIrange{4.8}{6.0}{\metre} off where the closure-free
runs hold half a metre, and a vertical drift on the same layout with traffic
that only an accepted closure removes (\cref{tab:realistic-baseline}). The
front end changes throughput, \SIrange{97}{99}{\percent} of frames kept with
the GPU extractor against \SIrange{72}{94}{\percent} with the CPU one, and not
accuracy. ORB-SLAM3's \SIrange{13}{19}{\degree} rotational RMSE is one
initialisation event per run, not its tracking; the median rotational error of
every run is under \SI{0.4}{\degree}. Eighteen repeated runs on the two mapping recordings
measure what each pipeline costs: VINS-Fusion optimises in
\SI{44}{\milli\second} against ORB-SLAM3's \SIrange{211}{406}{\milli\second}
of local mapping and holds its state in a quarter of the memory, for more
than twice the processor time, about eight cores of one against three and a
half (\cref{tab:mapping-resources}). Neither pipeline is cheaper outright,
and the choice between them is a memory-against-cores trade.

On the real robot ORB-SLAM3 tracks every recording: a provisional
\SIrange{0.404}{0.480}{\metre} planar ATE on \repo{realv6_000} and
\SIrange{0.172}{0.268}{\metre} across the three \repo{realv7} recordings, all
sixteen runs scored with no repair applied and with the marker lever arm
unmeasured (\cref{sec:exp1-real}). The cause of the IMU staying uninitialised
for most of nine of the original \repo{realv7} runs is identified in the
upstream code, patched in the fork and now exercised on every run; it removes
the need for the scorer's frame repair rather than improving accuracy. VINS-Fusion has
no valid real run: its two defects, IMU delivery and the gravity norm, are
diagnosed and in part corrected. Loop closing changed nothing on the real
robot that the front end did not change more.

\subsection{Experiment 3: is the map usable?}

Where tracking is sound, the mapping pipeline is: the tiny-warehouse map is
\SI{96.6}{\percent} precise in what it marks occupied, recovers
\SI{89.5}{\percent} of the surfaces it observed, and contains no blind cells
and \SI{0.7}{\percent} proven false obstacles along an independently driven
route (\cref{tab:map-geometry,tab:map-drivability}); the drivability half of
that result is dated, because its independent route is lost. The four
closure-free maps of the realistic warehouse all fail the quality criterion
fixed before scoring: precision \SIrange{77.8}{84.8}{\percent}, observed
recall \SIrange{74.9}{82.5}{\percent}, false obstacles
\SIrange{3.8}{10.2}{\percent} against the static-twin probes
(\cref{sec:map-realistic}). The false obstacles are not thickened shelving:
they are phantoms laid during the inertial initialisation transient at the
spawn and wall bands displaced from their true lines, and the current
configuration makes the maps worse than the October one through three
parameters with separable signatures, the lower detection rate costing views,
the longer range laying wall phantoms, and the higher ground-height cut laying
aisle obstacles on the I layout. On the large warehouse the attempt failed
through tracking loss in repeated structure and its artefacts were not retained
(\cref{sec:map-large}).


\subsection{Experiment 4: can the robot navigate on it?}

Half answered. Seven live runs on 12 November 2026 in three of the four
realistic worlds track ground truth to a median of \SI{0.50}{\metre} over
hand-driven routes of \SIrange{21}{153}{\metre}, with a minimum clearance of
\SI{0.58}{\metre} and no collision; one run of the seven lost the pose and did
not recover (\cref{sec:exp4-sim}). The error is a constant offset rather than
scatter, it is largest on single-aisle drives and averages away over routes
that cross the building, and its magnitude matches the wall displacement
Experiment~3 measured cell by cell. The slot's accuracy is therefore
\emph{map-limited, not estimator-limited}, which says that the improvement to
chase is a better map. The same initialisation transient that lays
Experiment~3's blocking phantoms also latches the back end's first fix
\SIrange{73}{76}{\degree} wrong until it is held back for \SI{5}{\second}:
one transient, two symptoms, one prescription.

What remains planned is the half the experiment is named for. No goal was
commanded in any run, so nothing yet speaks to planning, to recovery, or to the
question the map scores raise but cannot settle --- whether a
\SIrange{3.8}{10.2}{\percent} false-obstacle rate blocks a route or merely
lengthens it. Every cell is a single run, and the acceptance sweep showed
run-to-run spread as wide as most differences between settings, so no
comparison within \cref{tab:exp4-tracking} is evidence.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

The limitations of \cref{tab:limitations} carry over without softening. Six
of them bound the conclusions above most tightly.

\begin{itemize}
\item Every simulated cell holds one run, so no between-cell difference is an
      effect; the one run-to-run figure in the report is the agreement of
      ORB-SLAM3's two front ends on where and how its loop closer fails.
\item No two recordings share a route, so traffic is a condition of a
      recording, not a tested factor.
\item Every result is from one laptop at one replay rate, and the
      mapping-recording repeats were not interleaved between configurations,
      so nothing here is evidence of real-time capability on the robot's own
      computer.
\item The non-visual baseline rests on idealised encoders in simulation and on
      a speed scale fitted against the truth on one recording on the real
      robot.
\item The detector is scored against projected geometry with exemptions, and
      its moving-object truth excludes the walking people.
\item The real-robot numbers are planar and provisional until the
      marker-to-IMU lever arm is measured, and rest on one tracker export that
      fails the validity rules and three that pass; VINS-Fusion has no valid
      real run.
\end{itemize}

The two hypotheses the project is named for, dynamic-object filtering and
dense mapping, have no real-robot evidence at all.

\section{Recommendations and Next Steps}
\label{sec:recommendations}

\textbf{Give Experiment~4 its first run.} Fix a goal set in each realistic
world, write the scorer that reads the Nav2 action results and the
ground-truth pose into the measures of \cref{sec:nav-eval-method}, and run each
world against each of its two map configurations. That is the one experiment
whose system exists, is launchable and has never been scored, and it is the
test the project's own title implies.

\textbf{Bound the loop closer.} Add a maximum correction against the inertial
drift bound, or a covisibility-consistency window, and test it on
\repo{realistic_001}, where the \SI{14}{\metre} false closure is reproducible
in both front ends.

\textbf{Fill the real-robot slots in the order the simulation results
suggest.} Exercise the fork's initialisation switches on a \repo{realv7}
recording and re-run VINS-Fusion with the corrected gravity norm; measure the
marker-to-IMU lever arm; replace the fitted wheel-speed scale by a measured one and record the
steering angle as a measurement so that the EKF can be formed with the
\SI{0.60}{\metre} wheelbase; annotate
a frame subset of the real recordings for the detector; and build a first
real-imagery database for Experiment~4.

\textbf{Tune the visual systems to the platform.} The cheapest remaining
improvement is a measured inertial noise model for the visual estimators:
Experiment~1 shows the noise model is worth more than the estimator.

\textbf{Start mapping after the inertial transient}, and reconsider the
November configuration's range and ground-height parameters, which account
for the realistic maps' false obstacles; score any map against a recording
that did not build it.

\textbf{Record every behaviour-changing switch in the run manifest}, refuse an
output directory whose name disagrees with its dataset, repeat every
nondeterministic configuration at least three times, and retain the artefacts
of failed attempts.

\textbf{Future direction.} Integrated relocalisation against a saved map,
global pose-graph optimisation with a bounded loop closer, map reconstruction
after objects have been moved, and alignment and merging of maps across
sessions remain future work; path planning stays outside the project.
