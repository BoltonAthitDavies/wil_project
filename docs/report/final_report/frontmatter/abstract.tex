\begin{center}Abstract\end{center}
\vspace{2mm}

Autonomous mobile robots in warehouses are today navigated with 2-D LiDAR and
QR-code infrastructure, which is costly to install and inflexible when the
warehouse changes. This project builds and evaluates a localization and mapping
pipeline that uses only stereo cameras and an inertial measurement unit: a
stereo-inertial front end, ORB-SLAM3 or VINS-Fusion, behind an RTAB-Map back end
that builds a prior map and relocalizes the robot in it, with outputs in the
form the Nav2 navigation stack consumes. It is evaluated in a Gazebo
simulation of a warehouse in two layouts, with and without moving traffic, and
on the real robot, in two experiments: an estimator comparison against
non-visual baselines, and visual-inertial odometry on a prior map.

In simulation VINS-Fusion localizes to \SIrange{0.22}{0.66}{\metre} aligned
ATE and ORB-SLAM3 to \SIrange{0.14}{0.52}{\metre} in eleven runs of sixteen;
four of ORB-SLAM3's five divergences follow from its loop-closer state, led
by a false closure between repeated aisles. A wheel-plus-gyroscope extended Kalman filter
trails VINS-Fusion on the visual systems' own inertial noise model and would
lead it on three recordings of four with a measured one, so the noise model
matters more than the estimator. Traffic
breaks neither front end and drives none of the divergences; the one
dangerous case, a vehicle leading the robot, raises the local error two- to
eightfold for four seconds and leaves under \SI{0.1}{\metre} in the
accumulated error. On four real-robot recordings, one of them a diagnostic, ORB-SLAM3 holds
\SIrange{0.19}{0.48}{\metre}, in the same accuracy class as wheel-plus-gyroscope
dead reckoning, and VINS-Fusion \SIrange{0.37}{0.79}{\metre} once its fisheye
field of view is masked. Measured per second of operation the real rig drifts
1.9 times as fast as the simulation, against 10.5 times per metre, because it
moved at a fifth of the working speed and often stood still. Every visual run meets the
\SI{0.1}{\metre} relative-pose target in both domains, on jump-free pairs and, on the robot, at
that slower speed; the
\SI{0.05}{\metre} ATE target is met by no run. The tiny-warehouse map passes the map targets, with
\SI{96.6}{\percent} precision and \SI{0.7}{\percent} false obstacles; the two
realistic-warehouse priors fail them, with phantom obstacles laid during the
inertial initialization and wall bands displaced by drift. Relocalized on those maps the robot tracks ground
truth to a median of \SIrange{0.13}{0.21}{\metre}, an error bounded in magnitude by
the map's own nodes. Real-robot accuracy is provisional until the
marker-to-IMU lever arm is measured, and the real-robot prior map remains
planned.
