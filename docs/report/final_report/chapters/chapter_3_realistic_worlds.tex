\subsection{Realistic logistics layouts}
\label{sec:realistic-worlds}

Two warehouse worlds were generated from the warehouse-design literature, whose
common prescription is a chain of zones --- receiving, storage, picking,
packing, dispatch --- served by cross-aisles, with fast-moving goods nearest the
docks and offices and charging out of the flow
\cite{asean2019warehouse,mecalux2024layout}. Both canonical flow shapes are
provided. In the \textbf{U-shape} layout receiving and dispatch share the north
wall, with rack storage in north--south aisles behind them
(\cref{fig:realistic-plan-u}); in the \textbf{I-shape} layout receiving is on
the north wall and dispatch on the south, so goods cross the building in one
direction (\cref{fig:realistic-plan-i}). Everything else is held fixed: the
same \SI{41.28}{\metre} shell, the same featureless floor, lights, physics and
robot spawn. Aisles are \SI{3.0}{\metre} between zones and \SI{2.8}{\metre}
between rack rows, and open dock doors show a docked trailer rather than the
sky. \Cref{tab:realistic-zones} lists what each zone holds.

\paragraph{Traffic and people.} The moving objects are four pallet jacks on
logistics jobs: each drives to a load, lifts it, carries it through the aisles
at \SIrange{1.2}{1.3}{\metre\per\second} and sets it down on a target slot,
in a rotation that loops indefinitely. Two move pallets between receiving,
block storage and dispatch; two move totes between the pick faces and packing.
The generator checks every leg and turn of every carrier, with its load,
against every other object before a world is written. Each world also carries
four walking people, two order pickers and two dock checkers
\cite{mir2024eaton}, on scripted routes at \SIrange{1.1}{1.2}{\metre\per\second}.
Vehicles and people are kinematic: they cost no physics, have no collision
response, and the estimators see them only as unlabelled motion.

\paragraph{Static twins.} Each layout has a static twin in which every object
is static and the traffic removed, with nothing else changed. A world and its
twin therefore share every footprint and differ only in whether the vehicles
and people move, which makes scene dynamics a within-layout condition rather
than a change of geometry.

\paragraph{Recordings.} Six recordings were made in these worlds
(\cref{tab:dataset-registry}): a static-twin and a traffic recording per layout
for Experiment~1, \SIrange{80}{125}{\second} over
\SIrange{103}{171}{\metre}, and one long mapping run per static twin,
\SI{374}{\metre} and \SI{385}{\metre} at a median
\SI{1.5}{\metre\per\second}, the project's design speed, which builds the
priors of Experiment~2 and carries the repeated resource runs of
Experiment~1.
