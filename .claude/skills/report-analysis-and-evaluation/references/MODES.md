# The ten modes

Each mode has a fixed output schema. Keep the field order: it is what makes two
analyses of different documents comparable. Every mode inherits the five rules
of the spine in `SKILL.md`; the "never" line under each mode is the extra
prohibition that mode carries.

Counts such as "ten items" are a ceiling set by the evidence, not a quota. Six
well-evidenced items beat ten padded ones, and the short list says why it is
short.

---

## 1. Key Information Extraction
**ดึงข้อมูลสำคัญที่ห้ามพลาด** · act as a professional document analyst

Select the information that changes understanding or a decision, rather than
summarising everything at equal weight. Up to ten items, ranked most to least
important.

For each item:
1. The information found
2. Why it matters
3. What is lost by missing it
4. How to use it
5. Page or section

**Never:** add information that is not in the document.

---

## 2. Hidden Insight Finder
**ค้นหา Insight ที่ซ่อนอยู่**

Connect material from different parts of the document to surface what it
implies but never states. Up to ten insights.

For each insight:
1. What was found
2. The supporting evidence in the document
3. How it connects to another part of the document
4. Why it is interesting
5. How it could be used

**Never:** blur fact and inference. Each insight states plainly which part is
quoted from the document and which part is the analyst's conclusion, with page
references for the quoted part.

---

## 3. Data Detective
**ค้นหาตัวเลขและสถิติที่น่าสนใจ** · act as a data analyst

Work through every number, percentage, statistic and table.

1. Select the ten most important figures
2. Say what each one means
3. Compare across periods or categories where the document allows
4. Identify outliers and trends
5. State the likely consequence of each
6. Present a summary table with page references

Where the data supports it, compute the percentage change **and show the
arithmetic** so the reader can check it.

**Never:** create a number. If a comparison needs a figure the document does
not give, say which figure is missing instead of estimating it.

---

## 4. Contradiction Finder
**ตรวจจับข้อมูลที่ขัดแย้งกัน**

Check the whole document for statements that disagree, fail to line up, or
would mislead a reader. Look specifically for:

1. Numbers or statistics that do not match
2. Passages that state different things
3. Conclusions not supported by their own evidence
4. Dates or periods that cannot both be right
5. Table contents that differ from the prose describing them

Output a table: the finding, the page, the nature of the difference, the
possible cause, and what to check next.

**Never:** manufacture a defect. If the document is consistent, say so
directly. A rounding difference that the printed precision cannot resolve is
not a contradiction; say that too.

---

## 5. Missing Information
**หาข้อมูลสำคัญที่เอกสารยังไม่บอก** · read as an auditor

Identify up to ten pieces of information that are absent or unclear, ranked by
importance.

For each:
1. The question the document cannot answer
2. Why the answer is needed
3. What the document does provide
4. What still has to be found
5. The kind of source that would provide it
6. The consequence of deciding without it

**Never:** conclude that the document is wrong merely because something is
absent. A document is only at fault for an omission it claimed to cover.

---

## 6. Risk and Problem Analysis
**วิเคราะห์ปัญหาและความเสี่ยง** · act as a risk analyst

Find the problems, risks and constraints that deserve attention, ranked by
importance.

For each:
1. The problem or risk
2. Supporting evidence from the document
3. The likely cause
4. The possible impact
5. A preliminary severity, with the reason for that rating
6. How it could be prevented or mitigated
7. Page reference

**Never:** present an inferred risk as a stated one. Keep two groups: risks the
document names, and risks the analysis infers.

---

## 7. Critical Thinking
**ตรวจสอบข้อสรุปที่น่าตั้งคำถาม** · act as a critical-thinking analyst

Test whether the document's conclusions follow from its evidence, neutrally.

1. State the document's main conclusions
2. Identify the evidence offered for each
3. Judge whether that evidence is sufficient
4. Name the assumptions left unstated
5. Offer alternative explanations that fit the same evidence
6. List what would have to be checked
7. Rate confidence, with the reason

Output as a table with page references.

**Never:** claim more than the evidence supports, in either direction. "The
evidence does not settle this" is a valid verdict and often the right one.

---

## 8. Opportunity Finder
**ค้นหาโอกาสจากข้อมูล**

Turn the document's content into up to ten opportunities to improve, develop or
build something, ranked by how realistically they could be acted on.

For each:
1. The opportunity
2. Supporting evidence from the document
3. Who benefits
4. How to start
5. The expected result
6. The resources it would need
7. The risks or limits

**Never:** present a proposal as a finding. Separate what the document shows
from what the analysis suggests.

---

## 9. Action Plan Generator
**เปลี่ยนข้อมูลเป็นแผนปฏิบัติการ**

Turn the findings into a 30-day plan that can actually be followed, in four
phases:

- Week 1: analysis and preparation
- Week 2: begin execution
- Week 3: monitor and adjust
- Week 4: evaluate and conclude

Output a table with: the period, the activity, its purpose, the expected
result, the proposed owner, the success measure, and the document evidence it
rests on.

**Never:** blur the plan's sources. Mark which rows come from the document and
which are the analysis's own proposals. Do not assign a named person as owner
unless the document names them; propose a role instead.

---

## 10. Decision-Making Summary
**สรุปเฉพาะข้อมูลที่ช่วยตัดสินใจ** · act as a strategy adviser

An executive brief, two pages at most, for someone who must decide and will not
read the whole document.

1. The decision to be made
2. The five most important facts
3. The numbers or evidence behind them
4. Three possible courses of action
5. The advantages and disadvantages of each
6. The risks to weigh
7. A preliminary recommendation, with the reason
8. What should be checked before deciding

**Never:** merge fact and recommendation. The brief keeps them in separate
sections, and the recommendation names the facts it rests on.
